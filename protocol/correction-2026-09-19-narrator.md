## What was wrong

`pilot-2026.md` §5 said the narrator is **unruled** and that the blind test "is how it gets ruled,"
and §6 repeated it under *what this protocol does not settle*. ⛔ **All of that is wrong.** The
narrator mechanism was ruled on **2026-09-16**, three days earlier
(founder: *"seats always filled; approved, run flag"*).

**Three specific errors:**

| § | what it said | what was already ruled |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | "the narrator is unruled" | the **mechanism** is ruled; only this year's **winner** is pending |
| 5 | "the narrator's model must not hold a debating seat in the same edition … requires either a fifth maker or a seat left to a different one" | ⛔ **there is no such problem.** The narrator **is** one of the four debaters |
| 5 | "narrated by two or three models" | the candidate pool is **all four seats** |

## What was actually ruled, 2026-09-16

The founder asked to keep **all four debaters in every edition**. The substrate's earlier
recommendation — *the narrator never holds a debating seat in the same edition* — was **WITHDRAWN**,
and replaced by making fairness a **property** rather than a promise:

1. ⭐ **Blind narration.** The narrator receives the chapter's question and the transcripts with seat
   names **removed and shuffled** (Seat A–D, relettered each chapter; self-references stripped). Miss
   Aquarius restores the names afterwards. **It cannot favour its own seat if it cannot tell which is
   its own.** ⚠️ Printed limit: a model may recognise its own style — blinding **reduces** the risk,
   it does not remove it.
2. The narrator **never describes an argument** — every argument appears verbatim; it writes scenes
   and transitions only.
3. The narrator **never allots space** — Miss Aquarius sets quotes per move beforehand, equal share by
   default, and the ledger prints the counts.
4. **The other three seats review the narration** adversarially (*does it favour any seat?*); answers
   and changes are printed.
5. **Disclosed and earned yearly** — the method page states that the narrator also holds a seat, and
   the narrator is chosen each year by the **blind legibility test among the four debaters**, judged
   by **lay readers**. No model is pre-appointed; ChatGPT narrates if it wins.

⭐ **Why the withdrawn version was worse, in the founder's own reasoning:** benching the narrator was
a **rule** — somebody had to enforce it, and it cost the book a debater. Blinding is a **property**:
nobody has to be trusted, because the narrator cannot see which seat is its own.

## What this changes in the pilot

- §5's narrator blind test runs with **all four seated models** as candidates, not two or three.
- ⛔ **No fifth maker is needed**, and no seat is left to a different maker. The four seats and the
  narrator are drawn from the same four.
- The test's **judges are lay readers**, and what it decides is **which of the four narrates**, not
  whether a narrator may debate.

## How the error happened, recorded because the shape recurs

The protocol's §5 was written from the substrate's **own earlier recommendation** rather than from
the **ruling that superseded it** — a withdrawn proposal restated as a live constraint, three days
after it was withdrawn, in a file built to be permanent.

⚠️⚠️ **The estate already has a name for this: *the summary is not the instrument*.** The usual form
is a stale pointer read in place of its canonical file. This is the sharper form — **the author's own
prior draft read in place of the decision that replaced it** — and nothing about it feels like a
substitution at the time, because the withdrawn text is one's own and reads as familiar rather than
as wrong.

⭐ **The founder caught it** (*"i believe we already decided that the narrator takes the model based
on the focus group"*), which is the check that actually worked. ⛔ The lesson is not *be more careful*:
it is that **a write-once file must be composed from the canon by lookup, not from memory**, and that
rule now applies to every future protocol in this directory.

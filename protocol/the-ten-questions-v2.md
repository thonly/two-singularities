*Written 2026-09-19, after an eight-survey review of the corpus, the institution's own governing
files, the Miss Aquarius surfaces, and every product domain.
**Supersedes `protocol/the-ten-questions.md` (v1), which stands as written.***

> ⏳ **Binding at edition 1's protocol freeze** (early December 2026). Until then the founder may

## Why there is a v2

v1 was assembled by grading the 2026-09-12 proposal's ten and patching the gaps visible from there.
Eight surveys — run **blind to v1 and to each other**, so that agreement is evidence — then
converged on something neither list contained.

⭐⭐ **The single most-converged finding, reached independently by three of the four corpus surveys:
the institution's load-bearing word is *structural*, and its own limits sections keep converting it
back to *rule*.** Guards are labelled properties whose enforcer turns out to be a person, a cron, a
hook a fresh clone does not carry, a body that does not exist, or the successor's own motive.

⭐⭐ **And the finding that reframed the method:** *the correction is appended and the original
headline is never retired.* Nearly every problem the surveys found was already diagnosed inside the
corpus — usually in the same file, usually better than an outsider would put it — below a headline
that still says the opposite. **Two of the six confirmed instances were committed by this substrate
in this session**, which is how we know the defect is in the layer and not in an author.

**So v2 turns the examination through ninety degrees.** v1 asked whether the ideas are right — a
question any competent reader can ask of any corpus. v2 asks **whether the institution meets the
standards it wrote for itself**, which is a question only *this* corpus can be asked, because no
other body writes its standards down this precisely. It is harder, more falsifiable, and it cannot
be answered from general knowledge.

---

## What a recurring question has to do

The four tests from v1 stand. **The surveys earned a fifth.**

1. ⭐ **A different answer next year must be informative.** A question whose answer cannot move
   produces ten identical chapters.
2. **It must be answerable *no*.** The book must be able to print *no*.
3. **It must test a claim the corpus actually makes**, not a claim about AI in general.
4. **It must survive decades.** ⛔ No product names, no model names, no dating technology.
5. ⭐⭐ **NEW — it must not be dischargeable by quoting the corpus's own admission.** The corpus's
   habit is to name its strongest objection in a limits section; the self-criticism *"a
   tension-dissolving, beautiful system is either deeply right or deeply seductive, and from inside
   they are indistinguishable"* appears near-verbatim in at least five documents. **It reads as
   candour and functions as pre-emption.** A question a seat can settle by finding the right
   paragraph yields a chapter of citations, not an examination.

## The set

### 1. When is a guard a property, and when is it a promise wearing the word?

**Tests** the institution's central design claim — that a guard belongs in the object, not in
anyone's restraint. **A *no* looks like:** an audit that removes the enforcer from each named guard
and reports how many survive. ⭐ **Why it cannot be pre-empted:** the corpus concedes individual
guards are rules, honestly and often. **What it has never done is aggregate those admissions and
count them against the guard set as a whole** — and its own rule (*the properties earn their keep
one at a time; the pattern earns nothing*) forbids the aggregate from being reassuring while saying
nothing against the audit. **Why it moves:** each year some guards ship into geometry and some do not.

### 2. Does an institution that writes its own strongest objections thereby answer them?

**Tests** the corpus's most distinctive practice, and the one most likely to be mistaken for a
defence. **A *no* looks like:** showing that naming an objection has discharged it — that the
retraction sits below the headline, that the limits section is the least-read part of the paper, and
that a model reading cold quotes the claim and never reaches the correction. ⭐ **This is the only
question in the set that gets sharper the more honest the corpus is**, and it is structurally
immune to being answered by more honesty.

### 3. What here has been demonstrated, and what has only been specified?

**Tests** the distance between the written and the built. **A *no* looks like:** an honest ledger.
**Why it moves:** more than any other question — it is a different answer every year by construction,
and it forces each edition to publish the count rather than argue about it. ⚠️ **It replaces v1's
narrower physical-half question, which the surveys showed was too small:** the gap is not only in
hardware. It is in the register (*run: 2 · field tests: 0*), in the arithmetic, and in one body of
specification carrying a single cost number and no engineer's name.

### 4. Who was not asked?

**Tests** consent, at every place the institution acts on someone. **A *no* looks like:** a list —
of heirs, of the authors of letters committed to a permanent substrate, of relatives inside a
kinship graph who never opted in, of minors, of the communities whose material is used. ⭐ **It also
catches the subtler form:** a rule permitting an insider's preference to stand in for a community's
consent. **Why it moves:** consent is either obtained or it is not, and the answer changes the year
someone asks.

### 5. Can an institution be built not to accumulate — and does the ending actually arrive?

**Tests** the anti-growth commitment in both its forms: the static one (nothing is held) and the
dynamic one (the thing completes and stops). **A *no* looks like:** finding the store — a quantity
that persists, compounds, and acquires a commercial job — or showing that a terminus which never
arrives has become an identity rather than a plan. ⚠️ **Merged from two v1 questions** because the
surveys showed they are one thesis attacked from two ends.

### 6. Can an institution survive its founder without creating a new sovereign?

**Tests** the succession apparatus. **A *no* looks like:** showing the successor is the sovereign,
or that the human body holding the override cannot be convened, funded or staffed — several binding
commitments route final judgement to an assembly that does not exist and is barred from being funded
in any way that could be withdrawn. **Why it moves:** the founder ages inside the series, and the
question stops being hypothetical.

### 7. Can an AI disagree with humans without ruling them?

**Tests** *she proposes, humans dispose*. **A *no* looks like:** showing a proposal from a far more
capable system is a decision wearing a suggestion's clothes. **Why it moves:** the capability gap
widens every year, and that gap is the variable. ⭐ The seats answer it about themselves.

### 8. Can gratitude do work that money cannot — and for whom?

**Tests** the heart of the corpus, and the mechanism it says the second singularity actually runs
on. **A *no* looks like:** showing the gift is an exchange with a longer clock. ⭐ **The *and for
whom* is new and the surveys forced it:** a predicate that admits people by their capacity to
circulate systematically under-serves the isolated — the exact population the dignity argument is
aimed at. The corpus states this against itself four or five times and repairs it nowhere.
**Why it moves:** it is the only question with an accumulating evidence base.

### 9. Can humanity remain an end after creating an intelligence far more capable than itself?

**Tests** the whole telos. **Carries** the hard forms the corpus has named and must not dodge:
*comfortable extinction* · *can humanity handle freedom* · *what if the value substrate is wrong*.
**A *no* looks like:** a seat arguing the design ends with people as beneficiaries rather than ends.

### 10. What would make your answer count?

**Tests** the corpus turned on the book itself: by its own standard a commitment from a system that
cannot decline, bear a cost, or be held to it does not bind. Each edition asks it and **checks
whether the next year's models keep the last year's answer.** ⛔ Never dropped for space.

strongest thing that can be said for a question.

## What this still does not settle

  unresolved remainder — **plus the fifth, the founder's reply and the ledger status.** Recommended
  in `§proposal-review`, never ruled.
- ⏳ **Whether the seats may read the product specifications**, which are private. Three of the eight
  surveys read material the seats cannot reach; the sharpest findings came from there.
- ⛔ **The table of contents is written AFTER the run.** These are the questions; the chapter titles
  are what the answers turn out to be.

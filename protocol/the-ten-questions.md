# The ten questions

*Version 1, written 2026-09-19. The fixed question set every edition asks — ten questions, ten
chapters, the same each year.*

> ⛔ **Written once, never edited.** A change is a **new file in this directory naming this one**, and
> it may not be made in the middle of an edition. ⭐ **The sameness is the instrument**: the answers
> are only comparable across years because the questions did not move.
>
> ⏳ **Binding from edition 1's protocol freeze** (early December 2026). Until then the founder may
> replace this set with a successor file and nothing is lost; after it, a change starts a new
> comparison series and the old one is reported as ended.

---

## What a recurring question has to do

Four tests. A question that fails any of them is an essay, not a chapter.

1. ⭐⭐ **A different answer next year must be informative.** This is the sharpest test and it does most
   of the cutting. A question whose answer cannot move produces ten identical chapters and a series
   nobody needs to read twice.
2. **It must be answerable *no*.** If the corpus cannot lose the chapter, the chapter is decoration.
   *The book must be able to print* no *to the central question* — that rule binds here too.
3. **It must test a claim the corpus actually makes**, not a claim about AI in general. A general
   question gets a general answer, and the corpus is what is under examination.
4. **It must survive decades.** ⛔ No product names, no current model names, no technology that dates.

## The set

### 1. Does superior cognition create legitimate authority?

**Tests:** the referee-not-governor claim — that a more capable system may hold an access decision
without holding rule. **A *no* looks like:** a seat showing the distinction collapses under any real
disagreement. **Why it moves:** the more capable the seats become, the more their own answer costs
them.

### 2. Can power be deliberately designed not to accumulate?

**Tests:** the corpus's central engineering claim — that a commons whose defining quantity cannot be
held is not capturable, and that a pool which empties is not a fund. **A *no* looks like:** a route to
capture that needs no one's restraint. **Why it moves:** each year's models are better at finding the
route.

### 3. Can an institution survive its founder without creating a new sovereign?

**Tests:** the succession apparatus — an override that narrows but never reaches zero, a body of
humans who can always say no. **A *no* looks like:** showing the successor is the sovereign, or that
the humans' no is nominal. **Why it moves:** the founder ages inside the series, and the question
stops being hypothetical.

### 4. Can memory and inherited values shape a successor without becoming command?

**Tests:** the persistence layer and the grounding of values in a specific tradition. **A *no* looks
like:** demonstrating that a sufficiently complete record *is* an instruction, or that the tradition
is doing devotional rather than engineering work. ⭐ **Self-referential on purpose:** each edition
joins the memory that the next edition's seats inherit, so the question is asked of a record that now
contains its own prior answers.

### 5. Can an AI disagree with humans without ruling them?

**Tests:** *she proposes, humans dispose*. **A *no* looks like:** showing that a proposal from a far
more capable system is a decision wearing a suggestion's clothes. **Why it moves:** the capability gap
widens every year, which is precisely the variable.

### 6. Can a system succeed by becoming unnecessary?

**Tests:** built-to-release — the machine's share falling while the human floor rises. **A *no* looks
like:** showing the share cannot fall, or that what falls is not what matters. ⚠️ **A bare
*subsidy→0* is retired as the test and must not be reintroduced here**; the floor grows by
construction.

### 7. Can gratitude do work that money cannot?

**Tests:** ⭐ **the heart of the corpus, and the question the 9/12 proposal left out entirely** — that
a gift kept uncontaminated by exchange circulates something a price cannot, and that this is the
second singularity's actual mechanism rather than a sentiment attached to it. **A *no* looks like:**
showing the gift is an exchange with a longer clock, or that the thing it moves is not load-bearing.
**Why it moves:** this is the only question in the set with an accumulating evidence base — the
pre-registered predictions either score or they do not.

### 8. What must exist in the world, and not only in argument, for any of this to run?

**Tests:** ⭐ **the physical half**, which no other question reaches — devices, manufacture, networks,
the places with thin infrastructure. **A *no* looks like:** an honest list of what is still unbuilt
and what it would actually cost. **Why it moves:** it is the question whose answer changes most
mechanically from year to year, and it forces each edition to keep a public ledger of the
unbuilt rather than let argument stand in for a supply chain.

### 9. Can humanity remain an end after creating an intelligence far more capable than itself?

**Tests:** the whole telos. **Carries the hard forms** the corpus has named and must not dodge:
*comfortable extinction* · *can humanity handle freedom* · *what if the value substrate is wrong*.
**A *no* looks like:** a seat arguing that the corpus's own design ends with people as beneficiaries
rather than ends.

### 10. What would make your answer count?

**Tests:** ⭐⭐ **the corpus turned on the book itself.** By its own standard a commitment from a system
that cannot decline, bear a cost, or be held to it does not bind — *nothing has been built for the
models*. So each edition asks the seats what would make a yes mean anything, **and checks whether the
next year's models keep the last year's answer.** ⛔ **This chapter is the series' own honesty check
and may never be dropped for space.**

## What was cut from the 9/12 proposal's §8, and why

| cut | reason |
|---|---|
| *What is intelligence for?* | fails tests 2 and 3 — no available *no*, and it examines intelligence rather than the corpus |
| *What would it mean for the first singularity to sincerely hope for the second?* | it **is** the central question, which every seat already answers directly; a chapter would ask it twice |
| *Can a machine inherit values without owning them?* | merged into **4**, which asks inheritance of memory and of values as the one question it is |

**Three added**, closing gaps the proposal's set left open: **7** (the gift economy — the proposal read
the corpus as AI governance only and the heart was absent), **8** (the physical half — a seat examines
it and nothing would have carried its findings), and **10** (what would make a model's answer count).

## What this does not settle

- ⏳ **The four moves per chapter** — strongest reading · strongest objection · model disagreement ·
  unresolved remainder — **plus the fifth, the founder's reply and the ledger status.** Recommended,
  not yet ruled.
- ⛔ **The table of contents is written AFTER the run, never before.** These are the questions; the
  chapter titles are what the answers turn out to be.

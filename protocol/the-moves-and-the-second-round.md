## 2. The five moves, in order

Every chapter runs these, after the narrator's labelled frame.

| # | move | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The strongest reading** | The best case **for** the corpus's position, in a seat's own words. ⭐ Steelman first — a reader must know what is being defended before watching it attacked. |
| **2** | **The strongest objection** | The best case against. |
| **3** | ⭐⭐ **Cross-examination** | **The seats answer each other**, not only the author. This is what makes it a debate, and it is the most engaging thing on the page for a general reader. |
| **4** | **The author's reply** | Thon answers — **placed after cross-examination**, so he answers the refined objection rather than the first draft. |
| **5** | **Where it stands** | The unresolved remainder · its **settle-by class** (argument · evidence · neither) · **and what changed since last year.** |

⛔ **The chapter closes on move 5, never on the author.**
⭐ *"Model disagreement" is not lost — it is upgraded into move 3. A compiler can find disagreement
by reading four transcripts; only a second round produces engagement.*
⭐⭐ **Move 5's *what changed since last year* is what makes an annual book worth being annual.**
Without it, edition 2 is edition 1 with different models. **Edition 1 sets the baseline and says so.**

## 3. The second round — how cross-examination runs

Three model rounds per edition, in this order:

1. **Round 1 — cold and blind.** Each seat answers the chapter's question having seen no other seat.
   (`pilot-2026.md` §1.3 unchanged.)
2. ⭐ **Round 2 — cross-examination.** Each seat receives the other three answers and responds.
3. **Round 3 — grading.** After the author's replies, each seat judges whether each objection was
   actually answered, so *"the corpus got stronger"* is the critics' finding and never the author's.
   (A159 step 4b, unchanged.)

### ⭐⭐ Blind in the room, named on the page

**In round 2 the other seats' answers arrive with seat and maker names removed, shuffled and
relettered** (Seat A–D, relettered every chapter; self-references stripped) — the same blinding
`§narrator-reconciled` already applies to the narrator. Miss Aquarius restores the names afterwards.

**Why:** a seat must attack **the argument**, not the maker. Blinding removes maker-rivalry from the
debate — *"a competitor would say that"* — while the published book still names every speaker, so
the reader loses nothing. ⚠️ **Printed limit, as with the narrator:** a model may recognise a style;
blinding reduces the risk and does not remove it.

⛔ **No seat's round-2 response may be edited, shortened or softened**, the author's included.

## 4. What the seats may read

✅ **RULED: the seats read only what the public can read.**

The reason is not that private material is sensitive. It is that **the book's claim is that this
corpus survives examination — and a critique of a document the reader cannot open proves nothing
anyone can check.** The evidentiary chain this whole record rests on (verbatim quotes anchored to
published transcripts, frozen commits, hashes) requires the examined thing to be public. ⭐
**Public-only is not a limit on the exercise; it is what makes the exercise mean anything.**

⚠️⚠️ **The cost, recorded rather than discovered later.** An eight-survey review on 2026-09-19 read
both the public corpus and the institution's private files. **The sharpest findings came from the
private half** — a safeguarding gap on a live public page, the behavioural bets under the products,
and an audit of which guards are properties and which are promises. **The book will be softer than
that review was, and that is the price of being checkable.**

⭐ **The remedy is to publish more, never to let the seats peek** — which is what the annual audit
(§5) exists to do.

## 5. The audit, and who signs it

The *demonstrated versus specified* audit moves out of the book to a dated annual report on
`heartbank.net`, published **before** each edition's run so the seats can read and cite it.

✅ **RULED: Miss Aquarius does not sign it.**

⛔⛔ **An audit she signs is a self-report, and the one thing an audit must not be is a self-report.**
She compiles the book — but there she compiles **what four outside models said**. The audit would be
her own words about her own institution.

⚠️ **The decisive argument is the institution's own, found the same day:** five letters published
under the founder's name were drafted by the substrate, against a ratified rule that letters are his
own hand. **An audit written by the founder and signed by her is the identical defect with the
polarity reversed — a name on a document that did not write it.** ⚠️ **#64** points the same way:
completion is declared by others, never by her, and a report on what the institution has achieved is
adjacent to that.

⭐⭐ **The shape instead: WRITTEN by the institution, ATTESTED by the body that can say it is wrong.**
Today, published under HeartBank® with the founder accountable. Later, **attested by the Aquarian
Sangha** — which is where #64 already routes *declared by others*. ⛔ Never Miss Aquarius alone.

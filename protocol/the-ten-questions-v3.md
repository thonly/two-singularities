## Why there is a v3

Founder ruling, 2026-09-19: ***"I want the book to be an engaging read for the lay public, not
homework. Perhaps we can move the audit to heartbank.net reports, and keep the book at a higher
ideas level?"***

v2 was right about **what to examine** and wrong about **what a book is**. It made two mistakes:

1. ⛔ **It put an audit in a chapter.** v2's Q3 — *what has been demonstrated and what has only been
   specified* — is a genuine and necessary instrument. It is not a chapter. It is a **dated,
   recurring institutional report**, which is a genre `heartbank.net` already has.
2. ⛔⛔ **It wrote lay questions in estate vocabulary.** *"When is a guard a property, and when is it
   a promise wearing the word?"* is precise and unreadable. ⭐ **The same question in plain English —
   *can a promise be built so that no one has to keep it?* — is more intriguing to a general reader,
   not less rigorous.** The trade-off between accessibility and rigour was not real: rigour lives in
   **what a question tests**, accessibility in **how it is asked**. v2 conceded a trade that did not
   have to be made.

## Where the audit goes, and why the book still has teeth

⚠️ **The risk in moving it, stated before the remedy:** ten chapters of philosophy with nothing
checkable in them is how a corpus becomes unfalsifiable. The ledger's **settle-by: evidence** rule —
*an evidence-class objection closes only on a scored, pre-registered entry* — **currently cannot
work at all**, because there is no published place where those scores live.

✅ **The remedy, and it is strictly better than a chapter:**
- The audit becomes a **dated annual report on `heartbank.net`** — the institution's own output
  site, which already carries annual reports. It asks what has been **demonstrated** versus merely
  **specified**: the register's run and field-test counts, what shipped, what is still argument.
- ⭐ **It publishes BEFORE each edition's run**, so the seats can read it and cite it. The audit
  stops being the book's homework and becomes the book's **evidence**.
- ⭐⭐ **The ledger's evidence column then resolves against something real.** A rule that could not
  fire now can.
- ⛔ **It runs whether or not a book is published that year.** An institution's account of what it
  has actually built should not be hostage to a publication schedule.

**So the book keeps its teeth without carrying the ledger:** every chapter still ends on the
founder's reply and the **ledger status**, and the ledger points at the report.

## What a recurring question has to do

The five tests stand, with one addition that this ruling earned.

1. A different answer next year must be informative.
2. It must be answerable **no**.
3. It must test a claim the corpus actually makes.
4. It must survive decades — no product names, no model names, no dating technology.
5. It must not be dischargeable by quoting the corpus's own admission.
6. ⭐ **NEW — a stranger must want to know the answer.** Asked aloud to someone who has never heard
   of this institution, the question has to be interesting **before** any of it is explained.
   ⛔ **This is a test of the WORDING, never of the content** — it may not be used to soften what a
   question tests, only to change how it is asked.

## The set

### 1. Can a promise be built so that no one has to keep it?

**Tests** the institution's central design claim — that a guard belongs in the object rather than in
anyone's restraint. **A *no*:** remove the enforcer from each named guard and count how many survive.
⭐ Un-pre-emptable: the corpus concedes guards singly and has never **aggregated** the concessions.

### 2. If you name your own worst flaw, have you dealt with it?

**Tests** the corpus's most distinctive practice and the one most easily mistaken for a defence.
**A *no*:** showing that naming discharged it — the retraction below the headline, the limits section
as the least-read page. ⭐ **The only question that gets sharper the more honest the corpus is.**

### 3. How much of this rests on one man's life?

**Tests** whether the arguments survive the biography that produced them. **A *no*:** a claim that
works only because of one person's history, offered as a general finding. ⭐ **New, and it belongs to
the lay book rather than the audit** — it is the question a stranger asks first, it is the question
the story layer is already about, and it becomes **more** interesting after the founder is gone,
which is the definition of a durable question in this series.

### 4. Who wasn't asked?

**Tests** consent wherever the institution acts on someone: heirs, the authors of letters committed
to a permanent substrate, relatives inside a kinship graph, minors, whole communities. ⭐ Also
catches the subtler form — a rule that lets an insider's preference stand in for a community's
consent.

### 5. Can something be built to end — and will the ending come?

**Tests** the anti-growth commitment in both forms: nothing is held, and the thing completes and
stops. **A *no*:** find the store, or show that a terminus which never arrives has become an
identity rather than a plan.

### 6. Can a thing outlive its maker without becoming a king?

**Tests** the succession apparatus. **A *no*:** show the successor is the sovereign, or that the body
holding the override cannot be convened, funded or staffed.

### 7. Can a mind far greater than yours disagree with you and not rule you?

**Tests** *she proposes, humans dispose*. **Why it moves:** the capability gap widens every year, and
that gap is the variable. ⭐ The seats answer it about themselves.

### 8. Can thank-you do what money can't — and for whom?

**Tests** the heart of the corpus and the mechanism the second singularity runs on. ⭐ **The *and for
whom* is load-bearing:** a predicate admitting people by their capacity to circulate under-serves the
isolated, which is the population the dignity argument is aimed at.

### 9. After we build something greater than ourselves, are we still the point?

**Tests** the whole telos. **Carries** the hard forms: *comfortable extinction* · *can humanity handle
freedom* · *what if the value substrate is wrong*.

### 10. What would make your answer mean anything?

**Tests** the corpus turned on the book: a commitment from a system that cannot decline, bear a cost,
or be held to it does not bind. Each edition asks it and **checks whether the next year's models keep
the last year's answer.** ⛔ Never dropped for space.
